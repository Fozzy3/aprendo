# Business model, legal and cost constraints for monetizing Aprendo (Saber 11 prep, Colombia)

Research date: 2026-11-05. Years are labeled on each finding. "Inference" means my reasoning, not a sourced fact. Not legal advice.

## 1. ICFES intellectual property: can released Saber 11 items be used commercially?

### Takeaway
No. The current (Feb 2026) ICFES booklets say "Todos los derechos de autor reservados" and explicitly forbid any for-profit use, direct or indirect, including transformation (adaptations, compilations, revisions); a 2015 ICFES legal concept names commercial PRE-ICFES programs as an example of illicit use. They are not Creative Commons. Any paid tier, and arguably any free tier that feeds a paid product, needs an original question bank.

### Cited Findings
- The Saber 11 Lectura Crítica question booklet (Feb 2026, ISBN 978-958-11-0926-5) carries "© Icfes, 2026. Todos los derechos de autor reservados." — [ICFES, Cuadernillo Lectura Crítica Saber 11, 2026](https://www.icfes.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/16-feb-cuadernillo-de-preguntas-lectura-critica-saber-11-2026.pdf)
- Same booklet, "Términos y condiciones de uso" (verbatim): "Queda prohibido el uso o publicación total o parcial de este material con fines de lucro. Únicamente está autorizado su uso para fines académicos e investigativos. Ninguna persona, natural o jurídica, nacional o internacional, podrá vender, distribuir, alquilar, reproducir, transformar, promocionar o realizar acción alguna, de la cual se lucre directa o indirectamente, con este material." — [ICFES 2026](https://www.icfes.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/16-feb-cuadernillo-de-preguntas-lectura-critica-saber-11-2026.pdf)
- The booklet defines "transformación" as adaptations, translations, compilations, updates, revisions, i.e. any derived work, which requires the owner's express authorization. It also allows quoting "the necessary passages" with attribution, as long as they are not so many as to be "una reproducción simulada y sustancial". Infringement "se perseguirá civil y penalmente". Misuse can be reported to prensaicfes@icfes.gov.co. ICFES logos are registered trademarks and may not be used. — [ICFES 2026](https://www.icfes.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/16-feb-cuadernillo-de-preguntas-lectura-critica-saber-11-2026.pdf)
- Older Saber Pro booklets (2018) say: "No se autoriza su reproducción, utilización ni explotación a ningún tercero. Solo se autoriza su uso para fines exclusivamente académicos." — [ICFES Saber Pro Razonamiento Cuantitativo 2018](https://www.icfes.gov.co/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/02_02_Cuadernillo_de_preguntas_razonamiento_cuantitativo_saber_pro_2018.pdf) (quoted via search snippet; not fetched in full)
- ICFES Concepto 2 de 2015: ICFES owns the copyright on its booklets and on "Examen Icfes Interactivo"; unrestricted publication on the web implies authorization for anyone to *access* them; whoever transcribes or uses them as basis of another work must cite the source; users "no podrán emplearlos con fines comerciales", and the concept gives commercial PRE-ICFES programs as the example of illicit use. Mere access is not grounds for a copyright action; an action needs proof of illegitimate, especially commercial, use. — [Concepto ICFES 2 de 2015 (normograma)](https://normograma.com/documentospdf/icfes2024/compilacion/docs/concepto_icfes_0000002_2015.htm)
- Commercial competitors market "simulacros tipo ICFES" or "formato ICFES" rather than official items: e.g. Seamos Genios advertises "more than 15 simulacros in ICFES format" from COP 15,000 per simulacro; Código U sells a season pass at COP 99,900. — [Seamos Genios](https://seamosgenios.org/); [Código U](https://codigo-u.github.io/) (via search summary)

### Inferences
- The "directa o indirectamente" clause means a free tier built on ICFES items that funnels users into a paid product (or a school licence) is also exposed. The safe line: paid features and school licences run only on original items; ICFES items, if kept at all, stay in a clearly free, non-commercial, attributed area, or are removed.
- "Transformation" includes revisions and compilations, so lightly paraphrasing ICFES items ("tipo ICFES" rewrites) is still a derived work. Original items written against ICFES's public reference framework (competencies, evidence, item format) are the standard route; copyright protects expression, not the test design or the competencies (general copyright principle, not verified against a Colombian source here).
- Using the ICFES name descriptively ("prepárate para el Saber 11") is common; using ICFES logos is not allowed.
- Enforcement risk is real but the observed market is full of "tipo ICFES" sellers; I found no reported lawsuit. Low observed enforcement is not permission; for a B2B seller, a school's procurement or legal review can raise this.

### Gaps
- Did not find whether ICFES grants licences to third parties for commercial use (on request). Worth a written query to ICFES.
- No reported enforcement cases or sanctions against prep companies found.
- Did not verify whether any ICFES document uses a Creative Commons licence; the 2026 and 2018 booklets checked do not.

## 2. Selling to schools and public entities: sales cycle, CAC, deal size, buyers and channels

### Takeaway
Hard evidence on Colombian sales cycles, CAC and deal size is scarce. What exists says: institutional cycles are long, rectors increasingly demand learning-impact evidence, public schools are hard to sell to because buying is centralized and political, and most LatAm education spend comes from families and private schools. Big public buyers (Bogotá, Medellín) run their own free Saber 11 programs, which competes with a B2G sale.

### Cited Findings

Buyers (directors, rectors, ministries) "cada vez exigen más evidencia de que la tecnología mejora los aprendizajes". — [Ecosistema Startup, EdTech en Chile](https://ecosistemastartup.com/edtech-en-chile-startups-que-innovan-en-educacion/) (via search summary)

- Comparable B2B price points (US, not Colombia): Khan Academy lists Khanmigo district partnership at USD 15 per student (minimum 250 licences, US only; undated page); a district contract shows a Khanmigo student-tutor add-on at USD 60 first-year vs USD 90 standard. — [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=18400); [district board contract](https://app2.boardontrack.com/org/X8qMBm/attachment/publicDownload/293326?s=X8qMBm) (via search summary; sources conflict, other aggregators say USD 10–35)

### Inferences
- Bogotá and Medellín, the largest B2G buyers, already give Saber 11 prep away for free with their own or contracted tools. B2G is likely a later, tender-driven channel (SECOP II), better aimed at smaller municipalities and Secretarías without their own program, and it needs a legal entity, RUT, experience certificates and impact evidence.
- Cajas de compensación are competitors in the B2C pre-ICFES segment (Colsubsidio sells a 96-hour course at COP 418k–1.18M). They are also plausible channels: a caja could subsidize a digital product for affiliates' children the same way it subsidizes its own course. No evidence found of a caja buying a third-party digital platform.
- Colsubsidio's price ladder (subsidized A vs non-affiliate D at ~2.8x) is a useful anchor for what Colombian families pay for in-person prep in 2025; a digital product sits well below it.

- No evidence found on the purchasing role of school associations (UNCOLI, ASPAEN, CONACED) or on publishers (Santillana, SM, Norma) bundling Saber 11 platforms with textbooks. Not searched in depth due to budget.

## 3. B2B2C: school recommends, family pays — legal limits in Colombia

### Takeaway
Legally constrained. In private schools, extra charges ("otros cobros periódicos") are voluntary, must be in the Manual de Convivencia approved by the Consejo Directivo and disclosed at enrollment, cannot cover mandatory course content, and schools cannot force a specific provider. For virtual platforms, schools may charge families only for the personal-use component. Fines run 50–200 SMLMV.

### Cited Findings
- Mineducación: private schools may charge matrícula, pensión and "cobros periódicos"; "otros cobros periódicos" (Decreto 1075 de 2015, art. 2.3.2.2.1.4) are sums for services other than tuition, transport, lodging and food; they must be set in the Manual de Convivencia, approved by the Consejo Directivo and communicated at enrollment. "Los otros cobros periódicos son de naturaleza voluntaria." They cannot cover common mandatory services (e.g. required computer, sports or English classes that affect passing a course). — [Mineducación, Útiles, uniformes y otros cobros](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/portal/micrositios-preescolar-basica-y-media/Educacion-Privada/Tarifas-y-Costos-Educativos/219219:Utiles-uniformes-y-otros-cobros)
- Resolución 18066 de 2017, art. 15: schools "no pueden incurrir en prácticas restrictivas de la competencia en materiales educativos"; they cannot require specific providers or brands. For textbooks a school may require a title, but not purchase from a specific supplier. For virtual platforms, schools may charge families only for the personal-use component, not institutional equipment (Decreto 1075, art. 2.3.3.1.6.9). — [Mineducación](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/portal/micrositios-preescolar-basica-y-media/Educacion-Privada/Tarifas-y-Costos-Educativos/219219:Utiles-uniformes-y-otros-cobros)
- Penalties: Ley 1269 de 2008 (amending art. 203, Ley 115) sets fines of 50 to 200 SMLMV and closure for repeat violations. Charges for parent associations are prohibited. Relevant circulars: 002 de 2026, 002 de 2023, 01 de 2016, 03 de 2014. — [Mineducación](https://www.mineducacion.gov.co/portal/micrositios-preescolar-basica-y-media/Educacion-Privada/Tarifas-y-Costos-Educativos/219219:Utiles-uniformes-y-otros-cobros); [El País Cali on Circular 002/2023](https://www.elpais.com.co/colombia/ministerio-de-educacion-prohibe-cobros-adicionales-en-colegios-padres-y-estudiantes-celebran-la-decision-2038.html)
- Observed practice (2026): private schools send circulars to grade-11 parents about pre-ICFES offers (e.g. Colegio Americano "Circular C 02 26.1 PREICFES"). — [Colegio Americano circular 2026](https://www.colegioamericano.edu.co/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/CIRCULAR-C-02-26-1-PREICFES.pdf) (title only, contents not read)
- Comparable regional rule: Guatemala's Mineduc said in 2023 that charging for digital platforms is not authorized. — [Prensa Libre 2023](https://www.prensalibre.com/guatemala/comunitario/ciclo-escolar-2023-mineduc-aclara-si-centros-educativos-pueden-hacer-cobros-adicionales-por-uso-de-plataformas-digitales/)

### Inferences
- A B2B2C model works in private schools only as an optional, voluntary product: the school may recommend it, the family buys directly, it cannot be required to pass a course, and it should be listed among "otros cobros" if the school collects. The cleaner setup is that the family pays Aprendo directly and the school gets the teacher panel. That avoids the school acting as collector.
- If the school wants it mandatory for all grade-11 students, it must pay from its own budget (B2B seat licence) or include it in approved tuition. That pushes toward the B2B seat model Aprendo already has (`seatLimit`, `licenseEndsAt`).
- Public schools (official) operate under free-education rules, so family charges there are not viable; public-sector access means B2G or sponsor-paid seats. (Inference; the gratuidad decree was not verified in this research.)

### Gaps
- No data found on conversion rates or complaint volumes for "school recommends, family pays" in Colombia.
- Did not read the full text of Circular 002 de 2026 or Decreto 1075 art. 2.3.3.1.6.9.

## 4. Universities and scholarships as demand drivers and sponsors

### Takeaway
Universities sell pre-ICFES courses rather than give them away (Unicauca, Sergio Arboleda, Icesi, La Salle all publish offers, 2025-2026). Score-linked scholarships (Generación E Excelencia, historically Ser Pilo Paga) create a high-stakes incentive, but the 2026 thresholds were not confirmed in official sources.

### Inferences
- Universities treat prep as a revenue line and an admissions funnel. A partnership pitch ("co-branded free diagnostic for grade-11 students in your region, leads to your admissions office") is plausible, but no evidence was found of a university paying a third-party platform for this. A sponsor would fund free student access, not pay per seat at private-school prices.
- Scholarship thresholds give a clear B2C message ("reach 350+") and a natural "until exam day" pass, but marketing must not promise scores.

### Gaps

## 5. LLM cost per active student and how platforms cap usage

### Takeaway
At current prices the tutor's model cost is small: a heavy user (200 tutor turns per month) costs about USD 0.10 on DeepSeek V4 Flash (Aprendo's current model) and about USD 1.00 on Claude Haiku 4.5. LLM cost is not the binding price floor; sales, support and original-content costs are. Caps still matter for abuse and for the HTML demo outputs, which are long.

### Cited Findings
- Aprendo currently uses `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash` via OpenRouter for both the tutor (tool calls) and lesson generation (`generateObject`); the code comment says it is ~1/20 of v4-pro's output price ($0.165/M vs $3.20/M) and that output dominates the bill. — `/home/war-machine/Documents/repos/aprendo/packages/convex/src/aiProvider.ts`
- OpenRouter DeepSeek V4 Flash provider prices (Nov 2026, USD per 1M tokens, input/output): DeepInfra 0.09/0.18; Fireworks 0.105/0.21; AtlasCloud and Parasail 0.14/0.28; Alibaba 0.15/0.30; Azure 0.21/0.56; cheapest listed Baidu Qianfan 0.047/0.095. — [OpenRouter DeepSeek V4 Flash](https://openrouter.ai/deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash)

The page notes Claude 4.x tokenizers use ~35% more tokens than older Claude models. — [Token Pricing reference](https://tokenpricing.pages.dev/pricing)

- Khanmigo family plan: USD 4/month or USD 44/year (held into 2026); teacher access free; no public daily message cap found. — [Khanmigo pricing aggregators](https://aitoolsatlas.ai/tools/khanmigo/pricing); [Khan Academy blog](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=18400)

### Cost model (inference; my assumptions, not sourced)
Assumptions per tutor turn: ~3,000 input tokens (system prompt, question, short history) and ~400 output tokens (short Socratic replies; HTML demos would be several times larger). Heavy active student: 200 turns/month = 0.6M input + 0.08M output. No caching discount applied.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| DeepSeek V4 Flash (mid-tier provider) | 0.14 | 0.28 | ~0.11 | ~1.1 |
| Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite | 0.10 | 0.40 | ~0.09 | ~0.9 |
| GPT-4o-mini | 0.15 | 0.60 | ~0.14 | ~1.4 |
| GPT-5-mini (excl. reasoning tokens) | 0.125 | 1.00 | ~0.16 | ~1.6 |
| GPT-4.1-mini | 0.40 | 1.60 | ~0.37 | ~3.7 |
| Gemini 2.5 Flash | 0.30 | 2.50 | ~0.38 | ~3.8 |

- Reasoning models (GPT-5-mini/nano, some Gemini modes) bill hidden reasoning tokens as output, which can multiply output cost; the table excludes them.
- Prompt caching of the fixed system prompt cuts input cost 50–90% on models that list cached prices.
- A typical student uses far less than 200 turns; a reasonable plan-level budget is the heavy-user figure as a ceiling.
- Price-floor implication: on DeepSeek V4 Flash, a capped tutor (e.g. 30 turns/day on paid plans, a much lower cap on free) costs well under USD 1.5 per student-year. Per-seat B2B prices are driven by sales and support cost and by the cost of writing an original item bank, not by tokens.
- Usage caps worth setting regardless: daily turns per student per plan, max output tokens per reply, a separate stricter cap on HTML demo generation, and per-organization monthly budget tied to `seatLimit`.

- Real Aprendo token counts per turn and per demo were not measured; the table should be recalibrated with production logs (OpenRouter usage per request).
- USD/COP rate not sourced here; convert at the current rate.

## 6. Pricing tactics with evidence

### Takeaway
Colombian prep is sold mainly as season or course passes tied to exam dates, with simulacros as the unit; in-person courses anchor high (COP 418k–1.18M), digital passes low (COP ~100k). Evidence on results guarantees, pilots and per-cohort vs school-wide pricing in Colombia was not found; recommendations below are inference.

### Inferences
- Per-cohort (grade 11, optionally grade 10) seat licence fits the product and the calendar better than school-wide flat tiers: buying is tied to the exam, and `seatLimit`/`licenseEndsAt` already model it. Licence end should run to results day (late Sept for calendar A), not calendar year end.
- A paid pilot or free semester lowers the rector's risk given the demand for impact evidence; the pilot's deliverable should be a measurable before/after diagnostic report. Free pilots must run on original items (see section 1, "lucro indirecto").
- Results-based guarantees ("sube X puntos o devolvemos") are risky: outcomes depend on the student, and advertising a promised score result may raise consumer-protection issues (not verified). Prefer usage-based or "money back if not used" guarantees.
- B2C "until exam day" pass priced between digital comparables (~COP 100k–300k) and well below in-person courses (COP 400k+). Freemium for students with a paid parent report is consistent with section 3 if the family pays Aprendo directly.
- LLM cost does not constrain any of these price points (section 5).

### Gaps
- No Colombian evidence on conversion rates of freemium prep, on per-seat school prices for Saber 11 platforms, or on results guarantees.
- No data on willingness to pay by school socioeconomic stratum.
